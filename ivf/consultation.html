Before you fill anything in

You are allowed to ask without committing.

Most people who contact a fertility clinic are not ready to book. They are trying to understand whether their situation is treatable, whether they can afford it, and whether the clinic will treat them like a person. Those are the right questions, and none of them require you to become a patient.

This first conversation is an assessment, not a transaction. We will ask about your history, your previous cycles, your budget and your fears. If we are not the right clinic for you — because of your diagnosis, your timeline or your location — we will say so and, where we can, point you somewhere better suited.

You do not need to have your medical records ready. You do not need to know which treatment you want. You do not even need to be sure you want treatment at all.

What we will never do

  • Call you repeatedly after you ask us not to.
  • Share your enquiry with a third party or a marketing list.
  • Quote a price on the call and change it after you commit.
  • Tell you that you must decide today to secure a place.
  • Promise a baby, a success rate or an outcome we cannot guarantee.

If any of this happens, raise it directly with the clinical lead. Not a generic inbox.

After you press send

Four steps. No surprises.

  1. 1

    A human reads it

    Usually within 24 hours on working days. If your message raises something urgent, we will say so and tell you what to do next rather than waiting for a scheduled call.

  2. 2

    A private 30-minute conversation

    By video or phone, at a time that suits you. You can bring your partner, a friend, or nobody at all. You can also ask to speak to a female coordinator.

  3. 3

    A written plan and a fixed quote

    What we would investigate, what treatment we would recommend, what it would cost and what is not included. In writing, so you can read it without anyone watching your face.

  4. 4

    You decide in your own time

    There is no deadline and no follow-up campaign. If you want a second opinion elsewhere, take one. If you decide to wait a year, tell us — the door stays open.

Discretion by default

Nobody needs to know you asked.

Fertility is private, and in many families it is complicated. You may not want your employer, your extended family or your community to know that you are exploring treatment. That is your decision, and we protect it.

You can begin with a first name or an alias. We will not confirm to anyone that you are a patient. Clinic visits are arranged through a private entrance where available, and your records are held under a confidential reference rather than your full name on a visible file.

If a relative calls asking whether you have an appointment, we will neither confirm nor deny it. If you want your partner involved, we will include them — with your written consent, and only to the extent you choose.

Your privacy controls

  • An alias is enough to start the conversation.
  • No marketing use of your story without separate, specific consent.
  • No sharing with third parties, including family, without your written agreement.
  • Private arrival and departure where the facility allows it.
  • Clinical notes stored to standard medical record-keeping requirements.
  • A direct route to the clinical lead if you believe your privacy has been breached.

Your enquiry is not added to a mailing list. There is no newsletter to unsubscribe from.

The difficult questions

What people are really afraid to ask.

These are the objections we hear most often. Each one deserves a direct answer, not a reassuring slogan.

“I am terrified of flying — and of being in a foreign country for treatment.”

Both fears are reasonable, and neither is dismissed here. Cairo is a short flight from most Gulf cities, and your coordinator handles the airport, the transfer, the accommodation and the clinic route before you land. You will have a named person on the ground and a written schedule, not a vague promise that someone will meet you.

If flying itself is the obstacle, tell us. Some patients begin stimulation at home under our consultant’s protocol and fly only for retrieval and transfer, which shortens the trip considerably. Others bring a companion. We will plan around the fear rather than pretending it is not there.

“What if it fails again? I do not think I can survive another negative test.”

We cannot promise it will work, and we will not pretend otherwise. What we can do is plan for a negative result before the cycle begins: who will call you, when, who will be with you, and what the first week looks like. You will not receive a template email and a payment link for the next round.

If you have already had a loss or a failed cycle, tell us at this stage. It changes how we prepare you and how we deliver results. It does not disqualify you from treatment, and it does not mean you have to decide about another cycle before you are ready.

“Will anyone find out that I contacted a fertility clinic?”

Not from us. We do not confirm or discuss your enquiry with anyone you have not named in writing — including relatives who call asking whether you have an appointment. You can use an alias, and you can ask us not to leave voicemails or send messages to a shared phone.

Your clinical notes are kept to standard medical record-keeping requirements. Your story is never used in marketing without a separate, specific consent conversation.

“How much does the consultation cost?”

The initial 30-minute conversation is free of charge and carries no obligation. If you later need a full consultant review of records, a written protocol or a second-opinion report, we will tell you the cost before any work begins. You will never be billed for a conversation you did not agree to pay for.

“Will I be pressured into booking a cycle?”

No. Our coordinators are not paid commission on cycles, and we do not use limited-time offers or “your slot is about to go” tactics. If we think treatment is not right for you now, we will say so — even though that means we do not get the booking.

If you feel pressured at any point, tell the clinical lead directly. That is a serious complaint, not an inconvenience.

“Can I speak to a woman?”

Yes. Your coordinator and your psychological fertility nurse are women, and you can request a female clinician for scans and consultations where the schedule allows. Tell us at the enquiry stage so we can arrange it rather than improvise on the day.

“My husband does not know I am asking. Can we still talk?”

Yes — we can have a first conversation with you alone. We will not contact him or anyone else without your permission. When you are ready to involve him, we can arrange a joint session, and we will not treat his absence as a reason to delay your questions.

If you are contacting us on someone else’s behalf, please only share their details with their consent.

“I am not ready to decide. Is it a waste of your time to enquire?”

No. A large share of the people who contact us are months away from treatment, and some never proceed. Understanding your options is a legitimate reason to talk to a clinic. We will answer your questions, send a written summary if you want one, and then leave you alone until you contact us again.

One conversation. No obligation.

Bring us the question you have been carrying alone.

Whether it is the money, the needles, the flight, the family, or the fear that hoping will make the fall worse — say it first. We have heard it before, and it will not shock us.
