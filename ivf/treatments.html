Scientific foundation

Four clinical pillars, working from one shared record.

Every Nile Smile cycle is built on four specialties that read the same file — not four departments passing you between them and re-asking the same questions.

Obstetrics & Gynecology

Ovarian reserve is mapped with AMH and antral follicle count on day 2–3, then matched to a stimulation strategy chosen from the full evidence-based range — Antagonist, Long Agonist, DuoStim or Mild / Mini-IVF. Where indicated we perform diagnostic and operative hysteroscopy, time the window of implantation with ERA / EMMA / ALICE testing, and deliver precision luteal support matched to your own hormone profile.

Internal Medicine & Reproductive Endocrinology

Before transfer we stabilise the systems that quietly decide outcomes: TSH and anti-TPO brought to a preconception target, insulin resistance and PCOS metabolic balancing, inherited and acquired thrombophilia plus autoimmune screening, vitamin D and micronutrient correction, and the chronic conditions — diabetes, hypertension, thyroid disease — kept under control alongside your own specialists.

Advanced IVF & ICSI Embryology

Your embryos are grown in an ISO-standard laboratory: ICSI, PICSI and IMSI sperm selection, Time-Lapse EmbryoScope culture, Day-5 / Day-6 blastocyst development, PGT-A and PGT-M screening, laser-assisted hatching, and ultra-rapid vitrification for anything you choose to preserve. Every decision is explained in language you can actually use.

Male & Female Reproductive Psychology

Structured emotional care for both partners — hormonal mood shifts during stimulation, needle and retrieval phobia, male-factor pressure and stigma, the two-week wait, and healing after a failed cycle. It is built into the cycle rather than sold as an add-on. See how our dedicated fertility nurse supports you both.

IVF and ICSI — the foundation, and the detail

In-vitro fertilisation is the substrate of everything we do. Your ovaries are stimulated to mature a cohort of eggs, those eggs are retrieved under sedation, fertilised in the laboratory, and cultured for five to six days until they reach the blastocyst stage. We aim for a single-embryo transfer and preserve the rest. ICSI — intracytoplasmic sperm injection — changes only the fertilisation step: instead of mixing sperm and eggs, the embryologist selects one sperm and injects it directly into each mature egg.

  • Baseline — day 2–3 bloods and scan confirm ovarian reserve, hormone profile and uterine readiness before a single drug is given.
  • Stimulation — 8–12 days of gonadotropin injections, monitored every 2–3 days so the dose is adjusted to your response, not fixed in advance.
  • Trigger — a precisely timed maturation injection so the retrieval lands in the correct 34–36 hour window.
  • Retrieval — a short, sedated procedure with immediate embryologist feedback on egg numbers and maturity.
  • Fertilisation — standard IVF or ICSI according to your semen analysis and prior history, not as a reflex upgrade.
  • Culture — Day-5 / Day-6 blastocyst development in a time-lapse EmbryoScope incubator, undisturbed and continuously imaged.
  • Transfer — usually a single best-quality blastocyst, with the choice explained and photographed for you.
  • Luteal support & test — precision-matched progesterone, then a blood test 10–14 days later.

Sperm selection goes beyond the standard when the history warrants it: PICSI selects sperm by physiological maturity (hyaluronan binding) and IMSI selects by high-magnification morphology. These exist for the cycles — usually male-factor, or where standard ICSI has previously under-performed — where the extra discrimination is genuinely useful. ICSI bypasses some of nature's selection, so we use it when there is a clear indication rather than as a default.

Genetic testing — PGT-A and PGT-M

PGT-A screens an embryo for the wrong number of chromosomes — a leading reason for implantation failure and miscarriage, and a risk that climbs steeply with maternal age. PGT-M tests embryos for a specific inherited condition, such as thalassaemia, sickle-cell disease or cystic fibrosis, when both parents are known carriers.

  • Biopsy — a few trophectoderm cells are sampled from each Day-5 / Day-6 blastocyst by an experienced embryologist.
  • Vitrification — the embryo is ultra-rapidly frozen while the genetics laboratory analyses the sample, so nothing is lost to a wait.
  • Interpretation — results typically return in 1–2 weeks; your consultant reviews them with you before any transfer decision.
  • Transfer — a chromosomally suitable embryo is warmed and transferred in a later, carefully prepared cycle.
  • PGT-M preparation — requires genetic counselling and laboratory probe design before stimulation even begins.

The limits matter. PGT-A is a screening test, not a guarantee: some embryos are mosaic — a mixture of normal and abnormal cells — and the interpretation is nuanced, with false positives and false negatives possible. PGT does not create embryos; if you have few, it may leave you with nothing to transfer. It does not remove the need for a healthy endometrium and a well-timed transfer, and it does not replace prenatal testing. We recommend it only where your age, history and embryo numbers make it genuinely worthwhile — and we tell you plainly when it will not change your outcome.

Surgical & uterine care — preparing the place implantation happens

A perfect embryo can still fail in a uterus that has not been assessed. Before we schedule a transfer we look inside, and we correct what we find in the same programme rather than sending you away to join another list.

  • Diagnostic & operative hysteroscopy — direct visualisation and same-session treatment of the cavity.
  • Polyps — removed, as they can interfere with implantation and are a common, correctable finding.
  • Adhesions (Asherman's syndrome) — divided and the cavity restored, often after previous surgery or infection.
  • Uterine septum — resected to improve implantation and reduce miscarriage risk.
  • Fibroids — assessed for cavity distortion; removed only where they genuinely threaten the outcome.
  • Endometriosis & adenomyosis — diagnosed and factored into the stimulation and transfer plan.
  • Endometrial receptivity testing (ERA, EMMA, ALICE) — to time the window of implantation and check the endometrial microbiome where the history suggests it.

We do not use surgery as a default. A hysteroscopy, a myomectomy or a receptivity test is recommended only where the evidence says it will change your plan — and we will tell you when the honest answer is to leave the uterus alone and focus on the embryos instead.

Ovarian stimulation protocols — matched to your physiology

There is no single "best" protocol. Your AMH, antral follicle count, age, BMI and any previous response choose the strategy — not a clinic template and not a fixed menu designed to upsell. These are the four we use most.

  • Antagonist — flexible and shorter, with the GnRH antagonist added mid-stimulation to prevent premature ovulation. The standard choice for the majority of cycles.
  • Long agonist — down-regulation from the previous cycle, useful for specific endometriosis, adenomyosis or synchronisation needs.
  • DuoStim — two stimulations within a single menstrual cycle, for urgent cases, low reserve, or where maximising egg yield against the clock matters.
  • Mild / Mini-IVF — gentler, lower-dose stimulation for low responders or where ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) safety is the priority.

The drug choice is individual too: recombinant or urinary gonadotropins, an oral progestin-primed option where appropriate, and a trigger selected from agonist, hCG or a dual trigger to balance maturity against OHSS risk. Where hyperstimulation risk is high, we plan a freeze-all cycle — vitrifying every viable embryo and delaying transfer to a calmer, prepared endometrium — rather than pushing a fresh transfer that puts you at risk.

Inside the laboratory

ISO-standard embryology, and the quality control behind it.

The embryology laboratory is where a cycle is won or lost, and it is the part of an overseas clinic most patients are asked to take on trust. Ours is run to ISO-standard practice: controlled air quality with HEPA and volatile-organic-compound filtration, stable temperature and pH in every incubator, and a documented, traceable process for every sample from collection to storage.

Each embryo has an unbroken chain of identity. Electronic witnessing and double-witnessing at every critical step — insemination, biopsy, cryopreservation and warming — make mix-ups structurally almost impossible, and every dish, patient and outcome is logged against a unique identifier. Culture media, consumables and gas are batch-tested before use, and incubators are continuously monitored for temperature, CO2 and humidity.

What this buys you is not a marketing claim but reproducibility: the same discipline applied to your cycle that a reference laboratory would apply to any critical biological sample — and the confidence that the numbers in your report reflect the embryos that are actually yours.

  • ISO-standard laboratory practice with documented quality control at every step.
  • Time-lapse EmbryoScope incubation, so embryos are assessed without being disturbed.
  • Electronic and double-witnessing of every sample — full traceability by unique identifier.
  • Batch-tested culture media and consumables, with continuous incubator monitoring.
  • Ultra-rapid vitrification with validated survival monitoring and annual audit of outcomes.
